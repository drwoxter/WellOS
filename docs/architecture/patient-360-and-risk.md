# Patient 360, Explainable Risk Engine and dMind Risk Agent

This document describes the Patient 360 view, the deterministic versioned
risk engine, the governed dMind risk summary, the `/risk` worklist, the
consultation-cockpit integration and the minimal insurer-facing risk
projection. It is the contract reference for migration
`0012_patient_360_risk.sql`, `wellos_domain::risk`, `dmind_gateway::risk`
and `routes/risk.rs`.

Everything here runs on synthetic data. The rules are an engineering
prototype of an explainable prioritisation aid; they are **not** a validated
clinical risk score and make no clinical, pricing, coverage or denial
decisions.

## Design principles

- **One source of truth.** Patient 360 composes the existing chart, Patient
  Brief, encounters/notes, service requests/observations, follow-up tasks,
  visits, internal alerts and care-team assignments. No clinical record is
  copied into a parallel model; the only new tables hold risk snapshots and
  their human review state.
- **Deterministic first, AI second.** Risk levels come exclusively from the
  versioned deterministic engine. dMind explains and cites; it can never
  raise, lower or hide a deterministic level.
- **No universal score.** Each of the seven domains and the overall result is
  a level (`insufficient_data`, `low`, `moderate`, `high`, `critical`) with
  factors, evidence, trend and data gaps. There is no summed or weighted
  number — a single figure would hide *why* and invite misuse.
- **Explainable by construction.** Every factor carries a stable rule code,
  a plain-language explanation key, the timestamp at which it was detected
  and the WellOS records (`record_type`, `record_id`, `observed_at`) that
  triggered it, so the UI can link to the source and an auditor can replay
  the evaluation.
- **Same governance as every other dMind capability.** Risk summaries are
  `ai_artifacts` (autonomy A2, awaiting review → approved / rejected /
  superseded), generated by the configured dMind provider (the
  deterministic fixture in development/test; unavailable when the provider
  is `disabled`), labelled as AI output and bound to the exact assessment
  they explain.

The migration creates new tables and nullable columns only. It never rewrites
or reinterprets existing clinical data. Rollback statements are listed at the
end of the migration file; dropping `risk_*` loses only derived snapshots and
review state, which can be recalculated (reviews cannot).

## Deterministic risk engine (`wellos_domain::risk`, `risk-rules.v1`)

`assess(&RiskInput) -> RiskAssessment` is a pure function of the patient's
records and `now`. The same input always yields the same output, and every
snapshot stores the `input_hash` of the facts it was computed from, so an
assessment can be reproduced and audited.

| Domain | Signals (rule codes) | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Acute safety | `open_critical_alert`, `visit_priority` (immediate/urgent arrival), `abnormal_vitals` (last 24 h) | Critical when an urgent alert is open or vitals breach the triage safety thresholds. Vitals count wherever they were recorded (triage or a consultation started without a visit); missing/stale vitals are only reported as a gap while the patient is in contact (open visit or in-progress encounter). |
| Chronic complexity | `multiple_chronic_conditions` (ICD-10 chapters for diabetes, CKD, heart failure, COPD, …), `polypharmacy` (≥ 5 / ≥ 10 active medications) | Stale monitoring results (> 365 d) are reported as stale data, not as a higher level. |
| Medication & allergy safety | `medication_allergy_conflict`, `duplicate_medication`, `allergy_status_unknown` | Conflict matching is by normalised substance name; `allergy_status_unknown` is a data gap, never a low level. |
| Diagnostic results | `critical_result_unreviewed`, `critical_result_open_loop`, `abnormal_result_unreviewed`, `abnormal_result_open_loop` | Reads the existing closed-loop state machine; an unreviewed critical result is always critical. |
| Preventive care | `hba1c_overdue` (180 d), `renal_function_overdue` (365 d), `lipid_screening_overdue` (5 y, ≥ 40), `blood_pressure_overdue` (365 d, ≥ 40) | Applied to patients whose conditions/age make the measure relevant. |
| Access & utilisation | `frequent_unscheduled_visits` (90 d), `repeated_no_show` (365 d), `no_recent_follow_up` (365 d) | |
| Care coordination | `overdue_follow_up`, `pending_result_overdue` (14 d), `unhandled_alert` (24 h), `no_responsible_professional` | |

Every domain result carries: `level`, `factors[]` (code, level, explanation
key, `detected_at`, evidence records), `missing_data[]`, `stale_data[]`,
`trend`, `rules_version`, `detected_at`, `calculated_at`. The overall level is
the maximum domain level; the overall trend compares the current and previous
snapshot (`improving`, `stable`, `worsening`, `unknown` when there is no
comparable history).

A patient with no encounters, visits, vitals, results or conditions is
`insufficient_data` in every domain rather than `low`: absence of data is a
finding, not reassurance.

### Fact collection (`collect_input`)

Currently actionable records are always loaded in full, regardless of age or
count: open alerts, open visits, non-superseded observations whose result
loop is still open, pending service requests, in-progress encounters and
open/overdue follow-up tasks (the Patient Brief's actionable statuses). Only
*closed* history is bounded — the 200 most recent closed visits (within 365
d), closed encounters and closed-loop results, plus the latest closed result
per analyte so preventive intervals see the real last measurement — and that
history only feeds frequency and freshness rules. A critical signal can
therefore never fall outside a cap.

When a patient has several open visits, the *current visit* is chosen by
operational precedence (`in_consultation` > `ready_for_consultation` >
`triage_in_progress` > `arrived` > `scheduled`), then recency; among bookings
the one nearest to now. A future appointment never masks an active arrival.

### When it runs

- On demand: `POST /patients/:id/risk/recalculate` (`risk.manage`).
- Automatically, inside the same transaction as clinically relevant confirmed
  changes: result ingestion and amendment, every closed-loop transition
  (review / notification / closure), and consultation note sign, vitals and
  diagnosis mutations (`recalculate_after_change`). Recalculation never touches the
  encounter, never blocks or interrupts an in-progress consultation, and
  records `risk.assessment.calculated` with the trigger.

### Review state and the safety floor

Acknowledge / mark-as-reviewed act on a specific `assessment_id` and domain
and are refused with `409 assessment_stale` when a newer snapshot exists. Because the review is bound to `level_at_review`, an acknowledged
`high` item that later becomes `critical` reappears as unreviewed. Low-risk
patients are hidden from the worklist unless `include_low=true`.

## dMind Risk Agent (`risk-summary.v1`, template `risk-summary@1.0.0`)

`POST /patients/:id/risk/summary` builds the request from the *current*
assessment plus the deterministic facts used to compute it, calls the
gateway (governed through `aigov`: reuse, quotas, consent; EN/ES),
validates the structured output and its evidence references
and stores an `ai_artifacts` row (autonomy A2, `awaiting_review`) bound to
`risk_assessment_id`. Persisted provenance: provider, model, model version,
route, the prompt version of the provider that produced it
(`risk-summary-deterministic.v1` for the fixture provider,
`risk-summary-openai.v1` for `openai_compatible`), schema `risk-summary.v1`,
input hash, generated-at. Reads return the stored prompt version; artifacts
predating stored prompt provenance fall back to `risk-summary-prompt.v1`.

The output contract (`RiskSummaryV1`):

```
risk-summary.v1 {
  schema_version, ai_generated: true, rules_version,
  overall_level, safety_floor, raised_to_floor,
  domains[]: { domain, level, summary, reasons[], cited_sources[] },
  missing_information[], contradictions[],
  follow_up_suggestions[]: { category, domain, text, requires_confirmation: true },
  limitations[], cited_sources[], confidence
}
```

Provider output is first **aligned to the deterministic assessment**: every
level is overwritten with the engine's level, and a proposal below the
deterministic level is recorded as `raised_to_floor` with the limitation
`ai_level_raised_to_deterministic`. AI can therefore never lower or hide a
critical signal. `validate` then rejects: wrong schema, output not labelled
`ai_generated`, `rules_version` mismatch, any remaining level disagreement,
domains missing or explained twice, empty explanations, a domain with
factors but no cited sources, a suggestion without `requires_confirmation`
or with empty text. Rejected output produces no reviewable artifact.

Human review follows the existing pattern: `POST
/patients/:id/risk/summary/:artifact_id/review` (`decision: approve |
reject`) refuses artifacts that are not awaiting review or whose assessment
is no longer current (`409 artifact_stale`). Only after approval may a
follow-up suggestion be turned into a task through `POST .../confirm`
(`suggestion_index`, optional `priority`), which requires an approved
artifact, refuses double confirmation (`409 already_confirmed`), records
`risk.suggestion.confirmed`, creates an ordinary `follow_up_tasks` row
owned by the confirming professional and recalculates risk in the same
transaction (open tasks are a care-coordination input). Because that
recalculation produces a new snapshot, the `summary` returned with a risk
section is the artifact bound to the current assessment or, failing that,
the most recent *approved* artifact of an earlier assessment, flagged
`for_current_assessment: false` so the UI can say it explains an earlier
assessment while its remaining suggestions stay confirmable. Awaiting
artifacts of older assessments are superseded and never shown. There is no
path from an artifact to an order, a prescription, a diagnosis, a source
record change or an insurance decision.

## Insurer-facing projection (`risk-projection.v1`)

`GET /patients/:id/risk/projection` is the *only* insurer-oriented surface. It
requires `risk.projection_read` (clinical administrator, or the
`insurer_integration_agent` service scope), purpose of use `operations`, and
the patient's `insurer_risk_sharing` consent. Every call — allowed, refused
or consent-withheld — records `risk.projection.accessed` with the outcome.

Exposed when consent is active: `authorization` (purpose, consent purpose and
status, `shared`), `assessment_available`, `last_updated_at`, and per domain
the level, trend, `rules_version`, `detected_at`, counts of missing/stale
data, review state and evidence provenance (`rule`, `record_type`,
`record_id`, `observed_at`). Without consent the envelope is returned with
`risk: null`. No names, notes, diagnoses, medications, results or free text
are ever included, and the response carries an explicit `non_goals` list
(`no_pricing`, `no_underwriting`, `no_coverage_decision`,
`no_authorization_decision`, `no_automated_denial`). Nothing in WellOS
evaluates eligibility, coverage, premiums, authorisation or denial.

## Permissions

| Action | Roles | Purposes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `risk.read` | physician, nurse, pharmacist, clinical administrator | treatment, operations, quality |
| `risk.manage` (recalculate, assign) | physician, nurse, clinical administrator | treatment, operations |
| `risk.review` (acknowledge, mark reviewed, dMind summary & review, confirm suggestion) | physician, nurse | treatment |
| `risk.projection_read` | clinical administrator; `insurer_integration_agent` service scope | operations |

All routes pass through the central `guard` (authentication → tenant → RBAC
→ service scope → purpose → facility → care relationship) and record the
authorization decision in the same transaction as the mutation. Patient 360
and risk reads require a care relationship or facility scope like the chart
itself; laboratory and research users are denied; unauthorised patients
return `404` (anti-probing).

summary lifecycle.

## Screens

- `/patients/[id]/360` — Patient 360. Everything important is on one screen:
  identity and demographics, care team and responsible professional, alerts,
  conditions, allergies and medications, recent encounters and notes,
  pending tasks/referrals/tests/follow-ups, diagnostic trends and abnormal
  results awaiting review, preventive gaps, current risk by domain with
  expandable technical evidence, risk evolution, the dMind summary panel and
  **Start / Resume consultation**.
- `/risk` — worklist for physicians, nurses, pharmacists and clinical
  administrators: critical and high first, filters, plain-language reason
  for every item, links to Patient 360 and the source record, acknowledge /
  assign / mark reviewed with audit. Loading, empty, error, unauthorised and
  insufficient-data states are explicit.
- `/encounters/[id]` — the consultation cockpit shows the **Patient 360
  before you start** card above the Patient Brief: elevated risk domains,
  alerts and pending items with evidence links, and the dMind summary panel.
  It re-reads after confirmed clinical changes (vitals, diagnoses, save,
  sign) and never blocks or overwrites the note. Creating a task from an AI
  suggestion needs the summary approved and an explicit confirmation dialog.

Risk levels are always shown with a glyph and text (`▲ Critical`), never by
colour alone; every badge, filter and disclosure is keyboard operable with
visible focus.

## Clinical governance before production use

- The rules, thresholds and ICD-10 prefixes are placeholders for clinical
  sign-off; version them (`risk-rules.v2`, …) rather than editing in place.
- Prospective evaluation of worklist sensitivity / alert burden.
- Evaluation harness for the dMind summary before a real model provider is
  relied on clinically; the fake provider is exercised in tests only.
- Hazards H-18–H-22 in the hazard log.

## Limitations and non-goals

- Not a validated risk score; no numeric score is produced or intended.
- No population analytics, cohort views or predictive models.
- The projection is a contract for future insurer integration; there is no
  insurer identity, delivery channel, export job or contract negotiation.
- Trend is snapshot-to-snapshot; there is no time-series modelling.
- Recalculation triggers cover the mutations in this repository; external
  feeds (medication reconciliation, appointment books) are future work.
